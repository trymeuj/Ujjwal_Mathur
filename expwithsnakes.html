The experiment with children and snakes in a room

BBC ran a popular experiment, introducing some infants with snakes. - Watch here

So, the experiment was conducted in a closed room, where the children could not see their parents/caregivers. The children were playing on the floor, and slowly, some snakes were introduced into the room, the snakes being trained and harmless, of course.

You might have seen a ‘sapera’ or snake charmer come with a snake to your locality, and some of us might have even touched the snake, and we knew of course that the snakes were harmless, unless we wouldn’t allow the man to enter the premises. Well, how do our younger siblings react to them? I don’t think they would have a fun interaction if they had to touch the snakes, right?

Guess how the newborns reacted to those snakes, or should I say interacted? Well, yeah. They interacted.

As soon as the snakes came around, even with their forked tongues, the young ones tried to find their caregiver for instructions. Where are they? They couldn’t find them, so they had to respond on their own. And well, they did. What was seen next was a lot of touching, and holding, and grabbing, and trying to lift the reptiles. And god, I didn’t expect that. In fact, I reacted very strangely.

The experiment along with several others made researchers conclude that we are born with no fears at birth. How do we get them?

Remember when the children tried to spot their parents? Yup, right there. Our caregivers, not only provide us with love, food, clothing, and shelter, but also what turn out to be our reflexes. A child, whenever in an unfamiliar situation, looks towards their parent, waiting for the instruction. And, that’s how we learn that we have to fear certain things. We keep up adopting behaviors from around to the point that one day we are fearful of being 5 minutes late to a meeting. By the way, we should be on time, but that’s not the point :). That’s how we even pick up our reflexes.

Fear is one of many emotions we feel, and one of many we pick up from our parents and people around us. This takes me to how a person’s childhood shapes their personality. For a long time, I kept this ‘fact’ in the basket of ‘western sycophants’ or ‘videshi chonchle’. But now that I realise, I just cannot not think about this whenever I see a low tempered or extremely angry guy (not being gender inclusive intentionally here).

I often think I would want to ‘cultivate’ the childhood experiences of my future children for this very reason. I will keep exploring.

Thank you for reading.
