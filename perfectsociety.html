What Would Happen in a Perfect Society?

A Utopia is a fictional or imaginary community or society that is considered to be near-perfect or have highly desirable qualities.

Would you want to be part of a Utopian world? I guess I would be. Until, I read about this experiment.

Several extremely smart people have imagined a Utopian world, what would it look like, what would be the dynamics of such a place?

The Experiment

Well, Biologist John B. Calhoun was one such gentleman. So curious, that he decided to build one. Just not for us, but for mice. Why mice? Because they share more than 98% of DNA with us. So, pretty close. Let’s build for them, and see what would happen if humans lived in one, he might have thought.

So, he built a paradise. A place with plenty of resources, food, water, shelter, regular cleaning, suitable temperature, everything but limited space, just as our Earth is. And he brought 4 couples there, selecting the healthiest mice he could.

The Rise and Fall

The experiment started and the species flourished, they enjoyed and started reproducing. By day 315, the population grew to over 600. Now, the resources were not exactly plentiful, and so the population growth slowed. There were more mice than there were social roles, which broke many of them down, and hindered their ability to form social bonds.

Now, there were two kinds of mice. One, that did not fight amongst themselves, and the other that did. Members of the first kind just ate and slept. While the second one, they fought for roles and reproduction, leading to widespread cannibalism, violence, and asexuality. The mouse society had collapsed.

By day 560, the population peaked at 2200. There were fewer pregnancies and the mice had stopped regenerating. The few left who could, the first group, had lost their social ability to do so.

"On day 920 was the last conception, and the last mouse died on day 1766, 4 years and 10 months after colonization."

Calhoun said the mice had died two deaths. One was that of their spirit and the society, and the other was of their physical body.

The Conclusion

A Utopian world led to overpopulation.

Calhoun concluded that overpopulation leads to violence and hypersexual activities, followed by asexuality, self-destruction, and extinction.

No matter how sophisticated we are, which we are contrary to mice, such a scenario leads to the same results.

Final Thought

I guess a perfect world is not so perfect.

Reminds me of a Japanese phrase from the bestseller Ikigai.

"Wabi-Sabi."
