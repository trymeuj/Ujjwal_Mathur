Perspectives

Credits: Andrew Bustamante, Lex Fridman, Henry Ford.

This is something I’ve come across several times.
Andrew Bustamante elaborated on the Lex Fridman Podcast about Perception vs perspective.
Henry Ford famously said,

If there is any one secret of success, it lies in the ability to get the other person's point of view and see things from that person's angle as well as from your own.

Very general statement, but is it that common in practice?
I used to think, “wow, that’s a great line, and I know that, so I guess I am smart”. Task Done.
I guess not. Knowing that doesn’t suffice the purpose of having that in practice. I observed myself, and figured out that I am far away from actually following the great advice by Ford.
I did an (inaccurate) analysis of my peers’ behaviour, and seldom did they show this trait. Only one of my many friends, who is a very humble and empathetic guy, showed it to some extent.

I also want to differentiate this from being empathetic as used in the general sense. Empathy is defined as “the ability to imagine how another person is feeling and so understand his/her mood”, and used in the sense of understanding someone when they are experiencing a high of emotions.

However, here I want to focus on understanding a person’s perspective in everyday situations—whether it’s a salesman selling laptops, a carpenter working, or a housemaid cooking.
These are everyday situations without emotional highs, and that’s exactly where Ford’s insight becomes crucial. Trying to mentally step into another person’s shoes and see the world through their eyes, is what he meant by understanding their perspective “of everything”.

As Bustamante said, Everyone has a perception of the world, their “Image” of the world, and it’s unique for everyone. But, there is no advantage in that, we don’t have any additional information about anyone, and therefore it doesn’t help us in “decoding” people and getting along with them.

However, if we try to understand a person’s perspective, their lens of looking at the world, and their reason/justifications of acting the way they do, that gives us extra information. Now that we understand the why of that person’s behaviour, we have an edge over others, and the use cases, as Bustamante asserted, are immediate and massive in the long run.

I believe this is extremely powerful and as beautiful in practice as it sounds. I will try to follow this for sure, and consciously keep myself in check.
I, Henry Ford and Andrew Bustamante personally recommend everyone to practice it a lot more.

Thank You for reading.
